Case Study 01
Military Parole-in-Place

He crossed the border twenty years ago with nothing. His son's service brought him home.

A father's two decades in the shadows ended by the one thing he never expected — his son's oath to serve.

Outcome at a glance
20 yrs
Undocumented before approval
April 2026
Green card received in person
+ EAD
Work permit filed in parallel
The situation

Twenty years ago, he walked into the United States without documents, without a plan, and without any guarantee that the risk would be worth it.

It was.

He built a home. He raised a family. He watched his son grow from a boy into a man, and then watched that man raise his right hand and enlist in the United States military. His son was willing to die for this country. And yet, after two decades of living here, working here, and building everything he had here, the father still had no legal right to stay.

He had no status. No work permit. No protection. And no idea that a path forward might actually exist for him.

He had accepted his situation the way so many undocumented parents do: quietly, gratefully, and without hope that anything would change.

He was wrong.

What we did

We identified a special humanitarian program available to the parents of active-duty U.S. military service members. It was a narrow window, and it came with a hard deadline tied directly to his son's service. Miss it, and the door closes.

We moved fast.

Our team built his entire case from the ground up. We navigated the legal complexity of his original undocumented entry — a factor that disqualifies most applicants under standard immigration pathways. We filed his green card application ahead of the deadline. And because we knew he could not afford to wait months without the ability to work legally, we simultaneously filed his work permit so he could support his family while the application was processed.

Identified military PIP eligibility Filed I-485 ahead of deadline Parallel-tracked I-765 EAD Documented son's active service

Every document. Every deadline. Every legal hurdle. Handled.

The result

Green card received, April 2026. He came to our office in person to pick it up. Work permit approved along the way.

Twenty years of uncertainty. Twenty years of looking over his shoulder. Twenty years of building a life he was never sure he would be allowed to keep.

Final outcome
His son served this country. Now this country has recognized his father's right to stay in it.

Case Study 02
K-1 Fiancé Visa

They fell in love in Jamaica. We brought them home to the same country.

A couple separated by an ocean and a visa system that does not care how much you love someone.

Outcome at a glance
K-1
Fiancé visa approved at consulate
Embassy
Extension secured before expiry
In flight
Adjustment of status now active
The situation

They met in Jamaica. The connection was immediate, undeniable, and complicated by one simple fact: she lived in the United States, and he did not.

Every visit had an expiration date. Every goodbye carried the weight of not knowing when the next hello would come. They were building something real, but they were building it across borders, and borders do not bend for love on their own.

They wanted what every couple wants. A home. A life. A future in the same place.

But getting there required navigating a process that most people do not even know exists — a process designed to be thorough, slow, and unforgiving of mistakes. They needed someone who knew the path. They found us.

What we did

We filed a K-1 fiancé visa on his behalf — the specialized visa that allows a foreign national to enter the United States specifically to marry their American partner. It is one of the most misunderstood and poorly handled categories in immigration law, and getting it wrong means starting over from zero.

We prepared his complete consulate interview package for his appointment in Jamaica. We kept the couple informed at every step so they were never guessing, never wondering, never left in the dark.

Then a problem surfaced.

The visa's validity window was shrinking, and his interview had not yet been scheduled. If the visa expired before the interview, the entire process would need to be refiled. Months of work and thousands of dollars, gone. We contacted the embassy directly, made the case for an extension, and secured additional time so nothing would expire.

Filed I-129F petition Built Kingston consulate package Negotiated embassy extension Initiated AOS post-arrival

No panic. No restart. Just the right team watching every detail.

The result

Fiancé visa approved. He arrived in the United States. They are getting married. And we are currently processing his green card application so he can stay permanently.

Final outcome
One couple. One firm. From opposite sides of an ocean to the same front door.

Case Study 03
Marriage Green Card · Bona Fides

They were young, in love, and had almost nothing on paper. We made it work anyway.

A couple with no shared lease, no joint bank account, and no years of photos proved their marriage was real with the one thing that mattered most.

Outcome at a glance
2 steps
K-1 → green card, same firm
Bona fides
Proven with what they had
I-751
Conditions removal next
The situation

They were young. They were just starting out. And by every metric the U.S. government uses to evaluate whether a marriage is real, they barely had a file.

No shared lease. No joint bank account. No five years of vacation photos. No co-signed car loan. No thick folder of utility bills with both names on them. Just two people at the very beginning of their life together, trying to convince a system built on paperwork that what they had was genuine.

The government has seen every fraud scheme in the book, and the burden of proof falls entirely on the applicant.

When you are 22 and you have been together for a year, the evidence that older, more established couples take for granted simply does not exist yet. That does not mean the relationship is not real. It means you need a legal team that knows how to prove it with what you have, not with what a checklist says you should have.

What we did

We started at the beginning. First, we filed the K-1 so he could enter the United States and marry his partner. That was approved, and they married. Then we filed for his green card — and that is where the real work began.

When it came time to prove their relationship was genuine, we got creative. We gathered every piece of evidence available: photos, messages, statements from friends and family, records of travel, anything that documented the reality of their life together.

But the strongest piece of evidence was one that no one could question.

A letter from her doctor confirming an early pregnancy. A life being built together. A family on the way. Not a performance for the government. Just the truth, documented by someone with no reason to lie.

K-1 fiancé visa first I-485 adjustment after marriage Affidavit-based bona fides strategy Medical record of pregnancy

We assembled the case. We told their story. We made the evidence speak louder than any checklist.

The result

Fiancé visa approved. Green card received. We are now filing the final step — the I-751 to remove the initial conditions on his green card and make his permanent residence official.

One firm. Every step. From the first visa filing to the final application, we have been with them through the entire journey.

Final outcome
They came to us with almost nothing on paper. They leave with a green card, a growing family, and the legal right to build their life here.
