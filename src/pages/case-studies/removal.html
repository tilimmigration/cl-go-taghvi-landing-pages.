Case Study 01
Asylum · Changed Country Conditions

She lived in the shadows for years. One uprising changed everything.

An Iranian student who turned a moment of global crisis into her path to safety.

Outcome at a glance
Asylum
Granted under changed conditions
+ EAD
Work permit issued
GC filed
Green card application pending
The situation

She came to the United States on a student visa. When it expired, she didn't go home.

She couldn't.

Iran wasn't safe — not for a woman who had tasted freedom, not for someone whose beliefs put her at odds with a regime that punished dissent with prison, torture, or worse. So she stayed. Quietly. Out of status. Year after year, living in a kind of invisible exile — present in America but frozen in fear, unable to move forward, unwilling to go back.

Then, in September 2022, everything shifted.

Mahsa Amini, a 22-year-old woman, was killed in the custody of Iran's morality police for allegedly wearing her hijab improperly. The country erupted. Protests swept across every major city. The government responded with lethal force, mass arrests, and executions.

The world was watching Iran burn. And buried inside that tragedy was a narrow legal opening.

U.S. asylum law allows applicants to file within one year of a significant change in their home country's conditions. The Mahsa Amini uprising was exactly that kind of change — and the clock was ticking.

What we did

We moved fast.

Our team built her entire case around that legal window — documenting the dramatic shift in country conditions, connecting the escalating danger directly to the specific risks she would face if forced to return, and filing her application before the one-year deadline closed.

We gathered every piece of supporting evidence. We prepared her thoroughly for her asylum interview — not just what to say, but how to tell the truth of a story she had spent years keeping silent.

Filed I-589 under changed conditions Documented post-Amini country conditions Tied danger to individual nexus Sensitive interview preparation

Every element of the case was airtight.

The result

Asylum approved. Work permit issued. Green card application now pending.

After years of paralysis — years of watching life happen from the margins — she lives in the United States with full legal protection and a clear path to permanent residence.

Final outcome
She's no longer hiding. She's building a life.
I-589 Granted

Asylum · Missed-Deadline Recovery

A notary nearly destroyed his case. We rebuilt it from the ground up.

A Venezuelan pro-democracy activist who almost lost everything — until the right legal team stepped in.

Outcome at a glance

Asylum at immigration court

The situation

He didn't run from Venezuela because he was afraid. He ran because the people who imprisoned him made it clear: next time would be worse.

Our client had been active in pro-democracy protests against Venezuela's authoritarian government. He organized. He showed up. He put his name and his face behind a movement the regime was determined to crush. In 2021, they came for him. He was detained and imprisoned for his activism.

After his release, he knew the people who had targeted him would not forget — and they would not forgive. He made the difficult decision to leave everything behind and seek safety in the United States.

When he arrived, he did what anyone in his situation would do: he went to a notary. They took his money. They told him they'd handle it. They didn't file it correctly.

By the time he found our firm, the damage was done. The government was arguing he had missed his filing window — that his chance at asylum had expired. Through no fault of his own, the very act of trying to protect himself had put his case in jeopardy.

What we did

We refused to accept that a notary's incompetence should cost this man his safety.

We dug into the procedural history and built a legal argument that the missed deadline was not his failure — he had done everything a reasonable person would do, and the consequences of someone else's negligence should not be held against him.

We gathered written statements from people who knew his activism firsthand in Venezuela. We compiled current country conditions evidence showing the ongoing danger he would face if returned. And we prepared him to testify before the immigration judge — to tell his story with the clarity and conviction it deserved.

Argued extraordinary circumstances Documented detention & activism Eyewitness declarations Full immigration-court testimony prep
The result

Asylum granted — June 2025. The judge accepted our argument, heard his story, and ruled in his favor. The notary's mistake did not define his outcome. The right legal strategy did.

He is now safe in the United States and beginning the green card process.

Final outcome
The activism that once put him in prison is now part of the record that set him free.

Asylum · LGBTQ+ Persecution

For the first time in his life, he doesn't have to hide.

An LGBTQ+ asylum case from El Salvador — where telling the truth was the bravest thing he'd ever done.

Outcome at a glance
Granted
Asylum in removal proceedings
EAD now
Work permit eligible immediately
GC in 1 yr
Adjustment of status next
The situation

He carried a secret across the border — the same secret he had carried his entire life.

Our client came to the United States from El Salvador legally. By every outward measure, he had followed the rules. But inside, he was holding something he had never felt safe enough to share with anyone: he is bisexual.

In El Salvador, that truth is dangerous.

The country's deep-rooted homophobia isn't just cultural — it's violent. LGBTQ+ individuals face targeted attacks, sexual assault, and murder. Law enforcement frequently looks the other way. For our client, the violence wasn't hypothetical. He survived sexual abuse as a teenager. He faced ongoing threats. He spent years suppressing who he was — even pushing himself into a relationship he didn't want — just to appear “normal.” Just to survive.

When he found himself in deportation proceedings in the United States, he faced an impossible choice: tell the truth about his identity — the truth that had nearly gotten him killed — or stay silent and be sent back to a country where that truth would follow him home.

He was terrified that speaking up would make things worse.

What we did

We created something he had never experienced: a space where he could tell the truth without fear.

Our team built his asylum case around his identity and the very real danger he would face if returned to El Salvador. We gathered country conditions evidence documenting the systematic violence against LGBTQ+ individuals in his home country. We documented his personal history with care and sensitivity — understanding that every detail he shared was something he had spent a lifetime learning to hide.

We prepared him to testify before the immigration judge. Not to perform. Not to exaggerate. Just to finally, fully tell the truth about who he is and what that has cost him.

Particular social group nexus El Salvador country conditions Trauma-informed declarations Immigration court testimony prep
The result

Asylum granted. He is protected. He gets to stay. And for the first time in his life, he does not have to hide who he is.

He is now eligible to apply for his work permit immediately and his green card within one year. But the document that matters most isn't on paper — it's the knowledge that his country of refuge sees him, believes him, and has chosen to protect him.

Final outcome
Telling the truth was the bravest thing he had ever done. It was also the thing that finally set him free.
