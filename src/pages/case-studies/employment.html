Case Study 01
EB-1A Extraordinary Ability

The world said it was the wrong time to be Russian in America. His talent said otherwise.

An extraordinary ability petition approved and green cards secured for a family of four during one of the most difficult periods in U.S.–Russia relations.

Outcome at a glance
EB-1A
Extraordinary ability approved
Family of 4
Green cards filed for all
Mar 2026
Case closed
The situation

When Russia invaded Ukraine, the world changed for every Russian citizen living abroad. Overnight, a passport became a liability. Suspicion replaced neutrality. Doors that were already difficult to open became harder still.

Our client felt every bit of that shift.

He is a highly accomplished scientist specializing in machine learning and data science — a professional whose research contributions and recognition in software engineering and artificial intelligence place him at the top of his field. None of that changed when the war started. But the way the world looked at his nationality did.

He was already on a temporary work visa that had encountered problems under a previous attorney. Now, with geopolitical tensions at a peak and Russian nationals worldwide facing heightened scrutiny, his immigration future felt more uncertain than ever.

The unspoken question hung over everything: can a Russian citizen realistically obtain a green card right now?

We believed the answer was yes. But only if the case was built so well that talent became the only thing anyone could see.

What we did

We built his petition around the one thing that transcends politics: undeniable expertise.

We filed under the extraordinary ability category — the highest standard in employment-based immigration. It is reserved for individuals who have risen to the very top of their field, and the burden of proof is significant. We welcomed that burden.

Our team documented every dimension of his professional impact. His published research. His citations. His peer recognition. His contributions to the fields of artificial intelligence and software engineering. We did not minimize his background or sidestep his nationality. We built a case so comprehensive that his talent was impossible to overlook, regardless of his passport.

We filed with expedited processing. The moment the petition was approved, we moved immediately — green card applications for him and his entire family of four, filed without delay. We also secured his work permit while the green card applications processed, so there would be no gap in his ability to support his family.

Filed I-140 EB-1A extraordinary ability Documented citations & peer recognition Premium processing requested Concurrent I-485s + EAD for family

Every step was sequenced. Every filing was precise. Every deadline was met.

The result

Extraordinary ability petition: approved. Work permit received July 2025. Green card applications filed for the entire family. Case closed March 2026.

A Russian citizen, at one of the most complicated moments in recent history to be Russian in America, is now a permanent resident of the United States. So is his wife. So are his children.

Final outcome
His talent was always extraordinary. Now his immigration status reflects it.
EB-1A Approved

Case Study 02
TN Visa · Canadian Professional

She drove to the border, handed over her file, and started her new job that same week.

A Canadian marketing professional approved for a cross-border work visa on the spot, first try, in a category where applications get denied every day.

Outcome at a glance
On the spot
Approved at port of entry
First try
No RFE, no second trip
Same week
Started new job in the US
The situation

She had the job offer. A U.S. company wanted her. The role was real, the salary was set, and the start date was on the calendar.

The only thing standing between her and the career she had been working toward was a border crossing and a visa category that rejects people all the time.

Our client is a Canadian professional, and there is a special cross-border work authorization available to Canadian nationals. But the category she needed to qualify under — technical writer — is one of the most difficult to get approved. The requirements are strict. The adjudicators are skeptical. And her background was primarily in marketing, not traditional technical writing.

If the fit between her experience and the visa category was not argued precisely, the officer at the border would deny her on the spot. No appeal. No second chance that day.

She needed more than a good resume. She needed a legal team that could connect every dot before she ever reached the window.

What we did

We worked closely with her and her employer to reframe the case around what her role actually required — not what her job title suggested.

As a Product Marketing Manager, her position demanded that she produce and oversee technical written content: website copy, sales materials, product documentation, and educational resources for a U.S. audience. That is technical writing. But unless the application made that connection explicitly, an officer reviewing it in real time at a border checkpoint would see "marketing" and reach for the denial stamp.

We drafted a precise job description that captured the technical writing requirements of the role without exaggeration or ambiguity. We gathered reference letters from previous managers that specifically highlighted her writing work and technical output. We packaged her education credentials and her ties to Canada. Every single piece of the file was designed to answer the one question the officer would ask: Does this person actually qualify?

Reframed role as TN-qualifying Drafted precise job description Targeted reference letters Border-officer-ready file

We did not leave room for doubt.

The result

Work visa approved at the border. On the spot. First try. She drove into the United States, started her new job that same week, and is now building the career she always envisioned. No delays. No requests for additional evidence. No second trip.

Final outcome
One shot. One file. One approval.

Case Study 03
E-1 Treaty Trader · Renewal

Five more years of building what he started.

A New Zealand entrepreneur secures a trade treaty visa renewal and keeps his U.S. business alive through 2030.

Outcome at a glance
5 years
Renewed through Dec 2030
Auckland
U.S. Consulate approval
Zero gap
In US market continuity
The situation

He spent years building something that works.

Our client is a New Zealand entrepreneur who operates a business that trades between New Zealand and the United States. He holds a specialized visa designed for nationals of countries that maintain a trade treaty with the U.S. — a visa that allows him to enter the American market, grow his client base, and build real revenue on both sides of the Pacific.

But that visa does not last forever. And when renewal time came, the stakes were not theoretical.

If the visa lapsed, he would lose his legal right to operate in the United States. The clients, the contracts, the market presence he had spent years building — all at risk because a piece of paper had expired.

Renewal is not automatic. The government treats it with the same scrutiny as a first-time application. The burden of proof falls entirely on the applicant to demonstrate that the business still meets every requirement: real trade, substantial volume, and ongoing operations between the two countries. He could not afford to get this wrong.

What we did

We approached the renewal with the same rigor we would bring to a brand-new filing — because that is exactly how the consulate would evaluate it.

Our team gathered updated invoices, contracts, trade records, financial statements, and profit and loss documentation to build an airtight picture of a business that is doing exactly what this visa requires: real, substantial, and continuous trade between the United States and New Zealand.

We prepared the full application package and coordinated directly with the U.S. Consulate in Auckland. Then we ran a thorough interview preparation session before his appointment, walking him through every question the consular officer was likely to ask, so nothing would catch him off guard.

Treated renewal as new filing Rebuilt trade-volume evidence Coordinated with Auckland consulate Full interview prep session

No gaps. No guesswork. No last-minute scrambling.

The result

Trade visa renewed and approved at the U.S. Consulate in Auckland, December 2025. Valid through December 2030.

Five more years to grow the business. Five more years to serve his U.S. clients. Five more years of the market access he spent a decade earning.

Final outcome
He walked into that consulate with a file that left nothing to chance. He walked out with half a decade of certainty.
